When reporting needs to support a sharper board conversation

A board package can contain the right metrics and still leave the difficult questions unanswered: why spending moved, whether booked revenue is live, how customer concentration affects the interpretation, or what a revised hiring plan means for cash.

The work is to make those questions discussable. We organize the evidence, explain variances and assumptions, and distinguish a reporting update from a decision the leadership team needs the board to consider.

The work we help you own

Build a consistent financial package

Connect financial statements, budget-to-actual analysis, forecast updates, and the cash view. Use reporting periods and definitions that the team can explain.

Make operating metrics interpretable

Work with the available customer and sales data. Clarify booked versus live revenue where relevant, disclose customer concentration, and document definitions and limitations.

Prepare the decision discussion

Identify the variances and assumptions that deserve attention. Organize the options, supporting evidence, and the question the leadership team needs to resolve.

Maintain investor communication

Coordinate reporting dates, investor updates, requests, and follow-up responsibilities. Keep the operating model and recurring communication aligned.

Working outputs

These are practical materials the work can produce. We agree priorities and deliverables around your company and the engagement scope.

Board financial package
Financial statements, relevant operating metrics, budget-to-actual explanations, and an updated cash outlook.
Decision and assumption notes
The options under consideration, the evidence behind them, and the assumptions that would change the recommendation.
Reporting calendar and definitions
Agreed dates, source systems, metric definitions, owners, and follow-up responsibilities.

A decision this work can clarify

What should the board discuss after a budget variance?

Working inputs
Actual results, the previous forecast, hiring changes, collection assumptions, and customer data.
The analysis
Separate timing differences from changed assumptions, then compare the operating responses available.
A useful output
A succinct explanation of the variance and a clearly framed decision with supporting evidence.

How we begin

  1. Review the next board date, recurring investor requests, and the decisions the leadership team needs to discuss.
  2. Compare the current package with the books, operating model, customer schedules, and cash forecast.
  3. Agree the reporting scope, definitions, preparation calendar, and owners of follow-up questions.

Questions founders ask

Do you help with the discussion as well as the deck?
Yes. The financial work includes identifying the assumptions, variances, and decisions that deserve discussion, and helping the leadership team explain the supporting evidence. The materials should support a conversation rather than merely fill a template.
Which SaaS metrics belong in the package?
Use metrics the company can define and support with available data, tied to the decisions at its stage. Customer concentration and data limitations affect interpretation; a longer list of metrics is not necessarily a more useful package.
Can board reporting and investor updates use the same inputs?
They should connect to the same underlying records, model, and definitions. The level of detail and requested information may differ, but the team should be able to explain those differences.
What if our close and model are not ready on time?
We first identify the dependency and ownership gaps across bookkeeping, close, model updates, and reporting. Establishing a workable preparation calendar can be part of the broader finance engagement.
