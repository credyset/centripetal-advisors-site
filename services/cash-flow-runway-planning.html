Cash Flow & Runway Planning

Cash flow planning shows when money is expected to enter and leave the business. Runway planning connects that timing to operating choices, so a founder can see how hiring, collections, and spending change the cash position.

When the hiring plan and the cash forecast tell different stories

A headline runway figure can hide collection delays, annual contract timing, or spending commitments. A useful forecast makes the assumptions visible and distinguishes a timing problem from a change in performance.

What we help you own

Near-term cash visibility

Maintain a rolling cash forecast that reflects payroll, vendor commitments, expected receipts, and the actual timing of cash movement.

Scenarios tied to decisions

Compare operating scenarios with explicit changes in hiring, revenue, collection timing, or discretionary spending.

A review cadence

Refresh assumptions against actuals, explain variances, and agree which cash conditions trigger a decision or a revised plan.

What the work produces

Typical working outputs include a rolling cash schedule, a longer-range operating forecast, a scenario comparison, and a short decision log. A useful review answers what changed, why, and what action follows.

How we begin

We start with a conversation about your company, the immediate decisions, and who owns finance today. From there, we assess the current foundation and agree on priorities, scope, and a working cadence.

Questions founders ask

Why use a 13-week forecast alongside the budget?
A budget describes the operating plan. A rolling 13-week cash forecast focuses on receipt and payment timing. The two views should connect, but they answer different questions: planned performance and near-term cash availability.
Can this be part of an embedded CFO engagement?
Yes. These responsibilities connect to the broader finance function. The engagement scope is agreed around your company’s needs and can evolve as the business grows.
