Before a raise becomes a deadline

The challenge is often explaining what the next round will fund and how the business will reach those milestones under different operating assumptions. A coherent model needs supporting records, a clear customer picture, and people accountable for answering diligence questions.

Preparation supports a financing process. Customer traction, the team, investor fit, and the financing terms still shape the decision. We connect the financial work to those conversations rather than treating a completed data room as the finish line.

The work we help you own

Model the use of proceeds

Connect hiring dates, sales capacity, collections, and spending to the milestones behind the proposed raise. Compare the plan with a slower-growth or later-close scenario.

Prepare the financial evidence

Organize historical financial statements, revenue by customer by month, cap table records, and supporting schedules. Identify gaps, document explanations, and assign owners to unresolved requests.

Support the capital process

Help assess investor fit, prepare financial materials, coordinate questions, and examine financing scenarios. Investor introductions and process support follow the agreed scope and company context.

Carry the plan into operations

Use the same assumptions in the post-raise budget, cash forecast, and board reporting. Keep the use-of-proceeds plan connected to hiring and spending decisions after the round.

Working outputs

These are practical materials the work can produce. We agree priorities and deliverables around your company and the engagement scope.

Capital and operating scenarios
A model connecting the proposed raise to milestones, spending, cash needs, and alternative timing assumptions.
Financial diligence package
Financial statements, customer revenue schedules, ownership information, and an inventory of supporting evidence.
Diligence issue and request tracker
Open questions, responsible owners, required explanations, and the status of requested materials.

How we begin

  1. Clarify the financing objective, intended milestones, timing, and current investor conversations.
  2. Review the operating model, historical financials, customer schedules, and cap table for the questions likely to require follow-up.
  3. Prioritize the preparation work and agree how finance, leadership, counsel, and other specialists will handle requests.

Questions founders ask

Do we need an investor request list before starting?
No. We can begin with the existing financials, customer schedules, cap table, and capital plan. The requested diligence scope will evolve with the investor and transaction; starting early helps the team identify missing records and ownership.
Do you help with investor conversations as well as the model?
The role can include financial materials, investor questions, fit screening, and capital-process support. We agree that scope around the company’s needs; it is broader than delivering a spreadsheet alone.
Does a complete data room make us investor-ready?
A complete folder is useful, but investors still need to understand the business and the evidence behind the assumptions. Preparation means being able to explain unresolved issues and capital needs, as well as producing documents.
Who handles the legal financing documents?
Your counsel handles legal review and documentation. Centripetal supports the financial analysis, modeling, and coordination around financing decisions, with responsibilities agreed among the team and specialists.
