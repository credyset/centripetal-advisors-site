Venture Debt Readiness

Venture debt readiness means understanding whether a debt facility fits the company’s capital plan and preparing the evidence lenders need. Centripetal helps founders examine the financing alongside the cash model and other capital options.

Before additional capital becomes an additional obligation

Debt can change the timing of a financing need, but it also introduces repayment obligations and contractual conditions. The evaluation should start with the downside cash path and the company’s ability to operate under the proposed terms.

What we help you own

Evaluate the fit

Connect the proposed facility to specific uses of capital, operating milestones, and the expected funding path.

Prepare the lender view

Organize financial statements, revenue and customer schedules, forecasts, and the information behind lender questions.

Model the terms

Examine draw timing, interest, repayment, fees, and covenant scenarios with your legal advisors reviewing the agreement.

What the work produces

Working materials can include a lender information package, financing scenario comparison, repayment model, and open-questions list. Comparing terms is useful only when the assumptions and obligations are visible.

How we begin

We start with a conversation about your company, the immediate decisions, and who owns finance today. From there, we assess the current foundation and agree on priorities, scope, and a working cadence.

Questions founders ask

Is venture debt a replacement for equity?
The appropriate capital mix depends on the business and the proposed terms. Debt carries repayment obligations and may include covenants or other restrictions. It should be evaluated within the full capital plan rather than treated as interchangeable with equity.
Can this be part of an embedded CFO engagement?
Yes. These responsibilities connect to the broader finance function. The engagement scope is agreed around your company’s needs and can evolve as the business grows.
