Venture Debt Readiness

Twelve questions and seventeen diligence items to evaluate financing fit, repayment, and the evidence lenders may request.

By Centripetal Advisors Concept updated October 9, 2026

Twelve questions before a term sheet.

Venture debt introduces repayment obligations and may include covenants, draw conditions, liens, and warrants. The terms and underwriting approach depend on the lender and facility. SVB’s explanation of venture debt provides useful background.

What purpose would the facility serve?

Tie the capital to a milestone and compare the outcome with an equity raise, a slower operating plan, or another financing option.

What repayment source supports the plan?

Distinguish operating receipts from an expected future financing. Make the assumptions behind repayment explicit.

How dependable are customer receipts?

Use contract terms, billing frequency, collections history, implementation timing, and churn in the forecast.

How concentrated is the customer base?

Show the largest relationships and model the cash impact of a delayed renewal, termination, or late payment.

What is the forward burn trajectory?

Connect payroll, hiring, vendors, and collections to a maintained cash model instead of carrying last month’s burn forward.

What cash is restricted or unavailable?

Separate available operating cash from balances subject to restrictions or other commitments.

What happens when principal repayment begins?

Model actual draw dates, interest, repayment dates, fees, and any final payment. Compare the debt and no-debt cash paths.

What conditions govern access to the capital?

Read draw conditions, milestones, availability periods, and covenants with counsel. Undrawn capital may not be available when assumptions change.

How much downside can the company absorb?

Stress-test revenue delays, customer loss, slower collections, and spending commitments. Identify the earliest decision point.

How does existing financing affect the facility?

Inventory debt, liens, convertible instruments, consent requirements, and obligations that may conflict with new financing.

What does existing investor support mean in practice?

Separate a supportive relationship from a committed financing. Discuss what would happen if the next external equity round were delayed.

Who will own reporting and compliance after closing?

Assign responsibility for lender reporting, covenant calculations, notices, deadlines, and ongoing monitoring.

Seventeen diligence items to prepare.

Use this inventory to locate evidence and assign owners. Confirm the lender’s actual request list; having these items does not establish financing eligibility.

Connect the last actual period to the forward forecast. Keep revenue, hiring, costs, and cash schedules consistent, with an owner for each assumption.

Use a consistent chart of accounts and explain restatements. Separate recurring revenue, delivery costs, and operating expense categories.

Show operating, investing, and financing movements. Reconcile the cash movements to the change in bank balances.

Resolve stale balances and tie retained earnings and working-capital movements to the supporting schedules.

Tie the schedule to the ledger. Explain disputed balances, collection timing, and overdue amounts used in the forecast.

Identify unpaid invoices, accrued obligations, and payment commitments. Connect them to the ledger and cash forecast.

Label a compilation, review, or audit accurately. Ask the investor what level of work is required and agree the scope with your accountant.

Locate the signed agreements, amendments, billing terms, renewal dates, and termination provisions behind the concentration view.

Separate recurring and non-recurring revenue. Keep customer identifiers consistent with the contract and billing records.

Use current close dates and stage definitions. Explain forecast conversion assumptions and stale opportunities.

List principal, interest, maturity, security interests, covenants, and conversion terms. Ask counsel to review consent and priority issues.

Locate valuation work, approvals, grant records, and any outstanding questions for legal and tax review.

Include equity, options, and convertible instruments. Review dilution scenarios and discrepancies with the responsible specialists.

Coordinate entity records, approval requirements, and borrowing authority with counsel.

Explain differences between the operating plan shared internally and the package shared with a lender.

Agree the purpose, consent, and owner of reference conversations; avoid describing future support as committed capital.

Coordinate required disclosures with counsel and keep relevant documents current.

Compare the obligations alongside the capital.

Keep the debt and no-debt cash paths together. Ask what happens if the next round slips, a draw condition is not met, or repayment begins before the expected milestone. Review the agreement with counsel before treating capital as available.
