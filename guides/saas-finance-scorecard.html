HOW TO USE THIS

Eight categories. Score each 1–5.

  1. For each category, read the five descriptions and choose the one supported by your current records and operating practice.
  2. If you are between descriptions, use the lower one until the missing evidence is resolved. These are editorial review prompts, not external benchmarks.
  3. Complete all eight categories to see an 8–40 total and a breakdown. The categories have equal weight; one serious issue can matter more than the total.
  4. Use the lowest-scored areas to start a review, then adjust the order for the decision, deadline, and risk your company faces.

Adapted from Centripetal’s client diagnostic: eight categories, each scored 1–5 and summed out of 40. The descriptions here are simplified self-review prompts. CATEGORY 01

Legal

Entity structure, option plan adoption, employee and advisor agreements, applicable 83(b) elections, and IP assignment. Review requirements with counsel.

Score for Legal

Key agreements or approvals cannot be located; responsibilities for finding them are unclear.

Some records are available, but missing signatures, amendments, or approvals are not tracked consistently.

Core records are located and someone owns the open questions for counsel.

Records and relevant schedules have been reconciled with the responsible specialists; unresolved issues are documented.

A maintained record set, access process, and review calendar keep changes and outstanding legal questions visible. This does not certify legal compliance.

CATEGORY 02

Cap Table

Accuracy of current ownership, ability to model forward, option pool sufficiency, treatment of convertible instruments.

Score for Cap Table

Ownership records or underlying instruments are incomplete, or no owner is assigned to reconcile them.

Current holdings are tracked, but grants or convertible instruments still need reconciliation.

The current schedule is reconciled to the available instrument and approval records, with exceptions tracked.

Financing and dilution scenarios have documented assumptions, instrument terms, and a responsible reviewer.

Scenarios are refreshed when terms change and reconciled with the responsible specialists. A platform alone does not validate ownership or conversion calculations.

CATEGORY 03

Team

Coverage and accountability across transaction records, close, billing, collections, payroll, planning, reporting, and specialist work.

Score for Team

The founder carries most finance work and key responsibilities are undocumented. Identify which decisions or deadlines lack support.

Transaction work has coverage, but review, collections, planning, or exception ownership remain unclear.

Core responsibilities have named owners, with remaining gaps in oversight or handoffs recorded.

Responsibilities, review steps, and approval boundaries are documented and connected to the reporting calendar.

Ownership is reviewed as the company and team change. The staffing model alone does not establish effective coverage.

CATEGORY 04

Systems

Stack across 11 categories: accounting, expenses, payroll, benefits, banking, HRIS, FP&A, cap table, billing, AP, CRM.

Score for Systems

Reports depend on disconnected files, unclear sources, or duplicated entries.

Core tools exist, but manual handoffs and reconciliation responsibilities are unclear.

A repeatable workflow produces the current reports, with known gaps tracked.

Source systems, handoffs, and reconciliations are documented and assigned to owners.

Changes and exceptions are monitored; the workflow is reviewed against the next operating needs. Buying a particular tool does not establish this practice.

CATEGORY 05

Treasury & Banking

Account access, payment approvals, available cash, banking arrangements, and responsibility for reviewing controls.

Score for Treasury & Banking

Account access, payment authority, or the available cash position cannot be explained from current records.

Some account or payment controls exist, but coverage and ownership are incomplete.

Access and payment approvals have assigned owners; unresolved controls are recorded.

Access, approval rules, cash restrictions, and account arrangements are documented and checked against actual practice.

Controls and exceptions are reviewed as people, accounts, and cash needs change. Yield alone is not a measure of treasury control quality.

CATEGORY 06

Accounting

Cash vs. accrual, monthly close speed, reconciliations, revenue recognition, AP/AR processes, expense categorization (R&D / G&A / S&M).

Score for Accounting

Reconciliations, reporting basis, or close responsibilities remain unresolved.

A close process exists, but balances or classification decisions still lack reliable support.

The close produces consistent statements and reconciliations, with remaining issues tracked.

Accounting policies, classification, supporting schedules, and close responsibilities are documented for the company’s actual arrangements.

Changes and exceptions are reviewed with the accounting owner. This description does not predict an audit outcome or prescribe one revenue-recognition treatment for all contracts.

CATEGORY 07

Finance

Budget, projections, KPI tracking, investor data package, review cadence, peer benchmarking, capital plan, burn calculation.

Score for Finance

There is no current maintained plan, or assumptions cannot be traced to the starting records.

A plan exists, but comparison with actuals and responsibility for changes are inconsistent.

The team regularly reviews actuals against the plan and records important assumption changes.

Revenue, hiring, costs, and cash assumptions connect to scenarios, operating decisions, and the capital plan.

The team refreshes evidence and assumptions as decisions change, records the response, and assigns owners to follow-ups. This is an operating practice rather than a funding standard.

CATEGORY 08

Investor FAQ Readiness

Definitions and source evidence for recurring revenue, retention, concentration, sales efficiency, cash, and the financing questions relevant to your company.

Score for Investor FAQ Readiness

The presentation has headline claims, but their source schedules or definitions cannot readily be located.

Some headline schedules exist, with missing definitions or unexplained differences.

Relevant metrics have documented definitions, measurement periods, and current source schedules.

The story, operating model, and supporting schedules are connected, with material differences explained.

Evidence, assumptions, and open questions are refreshed for the actual process. These practices do not predict investor conviction or funding.
