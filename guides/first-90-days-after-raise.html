The First 90 Days After Your Raise

A post-close operating playbook for SaaS founders: what to set up before the capital starts deploying.

The wire hits. Now what?

The first 90 days after a raise are when the financial infrastructure either scales with the company or becomes the bottleneck that slows everything down later.

Most founders focus on hiring and product after close. The finance work that does not get done in this window — treasury setup, reporting cadence, board-deck structure, forecast model — becomes technical debt that compounds.

Week 1-2: Treasury and cash controls

Move the capital into a treasury structure that separates operating cash from reserve. Set FDIC-insured sweep accounts. Establish wire controls, dual-authorization, and fraud-prevention protocols before you are under pressure.

Week 3-4: Reporting and board cadence

Build the board deck template before the first board meeting, not during it. Establish the monthly close cadence, the KPI dashboard, and the narrative format the board will see for the next 12-18 months.

Week 5-8: Forecast and scenario model

Build a three-scenario forecast that connects hiring plan, cash, revenue, and gross margin. The model should answer "what changes if we hire faster / slower / differently" without a rebuild.

Week 9-12: Operating rhythm

By week 12, the company should have a monthly financial review, a rolling forecast update, a board-ready deck, and a cash-management discipline that survives the founder's attention moving to product and hiring.
