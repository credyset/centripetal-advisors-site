What should a board deck help the board decide?

A useful board deck connects management’s recommendation to operating evidence, the assumption that could change it, and the people who own the response. This builder turns the meeting focus into a preparation outline and discussion prompts.

For board meetings and operating updates. It organizes preparation; it does not generate presentation slides or verify readiness.

    Use this outline in your meeting preparation

    The evidence behind the outline.

    Use the six areas to connect the recommendation to its support. Detailed evidence and discussion prompts sit with each area.

    01 · Core evidence

    The decision and management’s recommendation

    Start with the specific choice, the recommendation, the alternatives, and the consequence of waiting. Distinguish a request for input from an item that needs a formal decision; confirm the approval process separately with the people responsible for governance.

    Evidence and discussion prompt

    Evidence to bring: Decision brief, alternatives, decision deadline, and the person responsible for bringing the recommendation.

    Discussion prompt: What changes if we choose the alternative or defer the decision?

    02 · Core evidence

    What changed since the previous meeting

    Use the same reporting periods and definitions to compare actual results with the plan and the previous update. Lead with the material change. Separate what the team can explain from what it is still investigating, and connect the change to the recommendation.

    Evidence and discussion prompt

    Evidence to bring: Actual-versus-plan bridge, prior meeting commitments, explanation of material variances, and unresolved causes with an investigation owner.

    Discussion prompt: Which change affects the plan, rather than only the explanation of last month?

    03 · Core evidence

    Cash, commitments, and the assumption that could move them

    Connect the cash position to dated receipts, committed payments, and choices still open. Show how the proposed decision changes those commitments. Identify the customer, hiring, or financing assumption that would cause management to revisit its recommendation.

    Evidence and discussion prompt

    Evidence to bring: Reconciled cash position with any restrictions identified, dated receipt and payment assumptions, and an explicit alternative timing case.

    Discussion prompt: Which commitment would we revisit first if the key receipt or financing date moved?

    04 · Core evidence

    The bridge between the story and the numbers

    State the definition, reporting date, and source for each metric used in the decision. Distinguish contracted recurring revenue, live recurring revenue, recognized revenue, and cash receipts where relevant; do not require different measures to equal one another. Explain their timing and scope differences. If a prior number changes, show the correction and its cause rather than silently replacing the comparison.

    Evidence and discussion prompt

    Evidence to bring: Metric definitions, source schedules, reporting cutoffs, and an explained bridge for material differences or corrections.

    Discussion prompt: Can another reader trace the headline number to its source and understand the difference from the financial statements?

    05 · Core evidence

    Operating owners and dependencies

    Ask each relevant leader to own the interpretation of their result, the proposed response, and the decision or help they need. Finance connects those inputs to the plan and checks consistency. Identify dependencies across teams instead of using the deck as a sequence of disconnected department updates.

    Evidence and discussion prompt

    Evidence to bring: Named owners for the affected operating inputs, cross-team dependencies, and the action each leader proposes.

    Discussion prompt: Who owns the response, and what must another team deliver for it to work?

    06 · Core evidence

    Decisions, follow-ups, and supporting evidence

    Close the discussion with the outcome, accountable owner, next check-in, and condition that would reopen the decision. Keep detailed schedules available behind the main narrative. Agree the pre-read timing with the board and label the reporting cutoff so readers know what the package includes.

    Evidence and discussion prompt

    Evidence to bring: Decision and action log, unresolved questions with owners and review dates, and clearly labeled supporting schedules.

    Discussion prompt: What will we check at the next review to know whether the chosen response is working?

    Illustrative preparation · no client results

    A signed contract supports a hire. Implementation moves.

    Suppose a new subscription is signed, but its start date moves and the expected receipt depends on an implementation milestone. The deck shows contracted growth; the hiring recommendation depends on the timing behind it.

    The request
    Ask for input on starting the hire now or waiting for the milestone. Show management’s recommendation and the consequence of each choice.
    The evidence bridge
    Keep contracted and live recurring revenue distinct. Connect the customer’s start and payment assumptions to the hiring commitment in the cash plan, with the reporting cutoff stated.
    The owner and next review
    Name who confirms the implementation milestone and who refreshes the cash assumption. Set the condition and review date that would reopen the hiring decision.

    The board conversation is about the commitment and its dependency. More revenue slides alone would not resolve that question.

    Add the evidence for the main discussion.

    Use the relevant focus alongside the core outline. A financing conversation in a board meeting still needs an operating recommendation; use the separate diligence framework for an investor evidence process.

    Operating plan & priorities

    The trade-off in the operating plan

    Show which priority receives resources, what is deferred, and how the recommendation changes the plan. Connect functional milestones to the cash and capacity assumptions they depend on.

    Evidence to bring: Current plan, proposed changes, dated commitments, and milestone owners.

    Discussion prompt: Which assumption would make us change the resource allocation?

    Cash & a spending commitment

    The commitment and its cash dependency

    Put the proposed commitment alongside dated receipts and obligations. Compare an explicit timing alternative, and show which choices remain reversible before committing spend.

    Evidence to bring: Dated cash view, payment or collection assumptions, committed versus discretionary spend, and the commitment deadline.

    Discussion prompt: What happens to this commitment if the receipt arrives later than planned?

    Revenue & customer dependence

    The customer change behind the revenue result

    Explain which customer starts, expansions, losses, or implementation delays drive the change. Separate live from contracted recurring revenue when relevant. Connect a material customer dependency to the operating and cash response.

    Evidence to bring: Customer-level movement schedule, metric definitions, implementation or renewal dates, and the resulting plan changes.

    Discussion prompt: Which customer assumption supports the recommendation, and what changes if it does not hold?

    Financing & the capital plan

    The capital decision behind the operating plan

    Show what the financing would fund, the milestones it supports, and the operating choices if timing changes. Separate confirmed commitments from intended financing. Use the diligence guide for the evidence behind an investor process.

    Evidence to bring: Use-of-proceeds plan, financing status and timing assumptions, obligations, milestone owners, and an alternative operating path.

    Discussion prompt: Which operating choice changes if the expected capital is delayed?

    A material miss or emerging issue

    The issue, what is known, and the response

    State the miss and its consequence early. Separate verified causes from hypotheses. Show the proposed response, its owner, and the decision or assistance needed; make the unresolved evidence visible.

    Evidence to bring: Extent of the variance, known versus unresolved causes, proposed response, and investigation owners and dates.

    Discussion prompt: What evidence would change our explanation or our response?

    Questions before you build the slides.

    Does the outline replace the existing board agenda?

    No. Adapt it to the meeting’s purpose and the board’s agreed agenda. Use the preparation list to find missing evidence, owners, or questions, rather than adding every possible metric to the deck.

    Who should own each section?

    Assign the relevant operating leader to the result and proposed response. Agree who checks the numbers, integrates the narrative, and maintains the action log. The tool does not allocate those responsibilities for your company.

    What changes for an investor update or fundraising pitch?

    An investor update may communicate progress and requests without a formal board decision. A fundraising pitch connects the capital ask to the operating story and supporting diligence. Use the Series A diligence guide for that evidence review.

    Source and related frameworks

    Stripe · Contracted recurring revenue, ARR, and recognized revenue supports the metric distinctions. The outline and discussion prompts are an editorial preparation framework for firm review; they do not predict what a particular board will ask.
