Do I Need a Fractional CFO?

Compare embedded fractional support with internal finance leadership by looking at the work your company needs.

On this page

  • Start with the work that lacks an owner.
  • Compare scope before comparing a title.

Start with the work that lacks an owner.

A fractional CFO can fit when the company needs senior judgment across cash, reporting, capital strategy, and finance operations, while the workload supports an embedded part-time engagement. An internal VP Finance or finance leader may fit when daily management and execution require a full-time owner.

Which decisions are waiting on finance?

List upcoming hiring, spending, financing, and board decisions. Identify the information and interpretation each decision needs, and who is responsible for providing it.

What does the existing team cover?

Separate transaction processing, close oversight, forecasting, strategic analysis, and management of the finance function. The gap is often accountability across the handoffs.

How much continuity is required?

Consider the recurring reporting cadence, availability needed during decisions, and the management load of an expanding finance team.

Is the need temporary or ongoing?

A single cleanup project differs from ownership of a maintained forecast and capital plan. Agree a scope that matches the continuing work.

What will the founder hand off?

Describe the decisions, workflows, and reporting responsibilities that should transfer. Specify where the founder remains the approver or decision maker.

What does a future internal hire inherit?

Document the model, calendar, responsibilities, and systems so the engagement creates a clean transition to an internal finance leader.

Compare scope before comparing a title.

Compare the responsibilities, availability, judgment, and team management required. Ask each option to describe its expected outputs and working cadence. Engagement pricing and hiring costs depend on the scope; obtain current proposals for your situation.
