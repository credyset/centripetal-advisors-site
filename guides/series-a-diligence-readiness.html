Series A Diligence Readiness

Fourteen questions. Twenty-four diligence items. A working framework for the evidence behind your financial story.

Fourteen questions behind the financial story.

Use the questions to find inconsistencies before they become a diligence deadline. Readiness means evidence that supports the business story; investor requirements differ.

Question 01

What is live ARR, and what is contracted but not yet live?

Separate recurring revenue from services and implementation fees. State the definitions used for ARR and CARR, reconcile the customer schedule to the model, and explain any difference from recognized revenue.

Bring the evidence: A revenue-by-customer schedule with contract, start, billing, and implementation dates.

Question 02

What changed in growth over the last quarter?

Show monthly, quarterly, and annual views. Separate new customers, expansion, contraction, and churn so one large contract does not obscure the underlying pattern.

Bring the evidence: A monthly recurring-revenue bridge with an explanation of material changes.

Question 03

What is driving cash burn?

Separate cash operating receipts and payments from financing. Explain timing effects and one-time items; reconcile the result to bank movements instead of relying on a P&L proxy.

Bring the evidence: A cash-flow bridge and a forward view of payroll, collections, and vendor commitments.

Question 04

How much cash is actually available?

Reconcile balances across accounts and distinguish operating cash from restricted or committed amounts. Use the same opening cash balance in the operating model and the board materials.

Bring the evidence: A dated cash schedule tied to reconciled bank statements.

Question 05

What changes the runway forecast?

Model hiring, collection timing, payment commitments, and the proposed financing separately. Compare a base case with a downside and identify when management would need to act.

Bring the evidence: An assumption log, scenario comparison, and decision triggers.

Question 06

How are retention and expansion measured?

Use consistent cohort boundaries and a defined measurement period. Explain gross and net retention separately, including which customers and revenue changes are included.

Bring the evidence: A customer-level bridge that can reproduce the headline metrics.

Question 07

What does logo churn hide?

Look at both the number of customers lost and the revenue attached to them. A small number of departures may have a large impact when the customer base is concentrated.

Bring the evidence: Customer counts, revenue lost, and reasons for departure using the same period.

Question 08

Where did the net change in recurring revenue come from?

A single growth rate hides the composition of the result. Make new revenue, expansion, contraction, and churn visible and trace them to the underlying customer records.

Bring the evidence: A monthly waterfall with consistent definitions and no double counting.

Question 09

How do contracts turn into cash?

Separate contract duration from invoice frequency. Show implementation timing, payment terms, renewal conditions, and collections assumptions in the cash model.

Bring the evidence: Sample customer contracts, invoice schedules, and an aged receivables view.

Question 10

What happens if the largest customer changes course?

Calculate concentration for the largest customer and the top five and ten customers using a stated revenue basis. Connect renewal timing, termination rights, implementation status, and collections to a downside scenario.

Bring the evidence: A concentration schedule, relationship context, and a cash sensitivity; avoid treating a generic threshold as investor policy.

Question 11

What explains the margin trend?

Show the distinction between subscription delivery costs, implementation work, and operating expenses. Identify one-time adjustments and keep the adjusted view connected to the books.

Bring the evidence: A documented margin bridge and consistent cost classification.

Question 12

Do sales-cycle assumptions match the pipeline?

Define where the cycle begins and ends. Compare actual stage progression with forecast assumptions and explain stalled opportunities or unusually long implementation paths.

Bring the evidence: A current pipeline and cohort view tied to the sales-capacity model.

Question 13

Does average contract value describe the customer base?

State whether the figure reflects annualized value, total contract value, or recognized revenue. Show the distribution so a handful of large customers do not disappear inside the average.

Bring the evidence: Customer-level contract values with clear term and revenue definitions.

Question 14

Which channels produce opportunities that become customers?

Connect channel activity to opportunities, wins, acquisition spending, and the timing of collections. Explain incomplete attribution rather than presenting false precision.

Bring the evidence: A channel view with conversion definitions, cost assumptions, and known gaps.

Twenty-four items to organize before diligence.

Check an item only when you can locate the current evidence and identify its owner. This is an organizing checklist, not a completeness certification.

Connect the last actual period to the forward forecast. Keep revenue, hiring, costs, and cash schedules consistent, with an owner for each assumption.

Use a consistent chart of accounts and explain restatements. Separate recurring revenue, delivery costs, and operating expense categories.

Show operating, investing, and financing movements. Reconcile the cash movements to the change in bank balances.

Resolve stale balances and tie retained earnings and working-capital movements to the supporting schedules.

Separate recurring and non-recurring revenue. Keep customer identifiers consistent with the contract and billing records.

Tie the schedule to the ledger. Explain disputed balances, collection timing, and overdue amounts used in the forecast.

Include equity, options, and convertible instruments. Review dilution scenarios and discrepancies with the responsible specialists.

Locate the signed agreements, amendments, billing terms, renewal dates, and termination provisions behind the concentration view.

Document ARR, CARR, gross retention, net retention, logo churn, and the measurement periods used in reporting.

Explain the material variances, which assumptions changed, and the management decisions that followed.

Connect start dates, compensation, role ownership, and delivery capacity to the operating model.

Show the bridge from signed contracts to live customers, billing, revenue recognition, and collections.

Locate valuation work, approvals, grant records, and any outstanding questions for legal and tax review.

Maintain a controlled-access view of roles, start dates, compensation, and equity commitments. Share only what the diligence process requires.

Review signed agreements and IP assignments with counsel. Track missing records, relevant approvals, and unresolved questions.

Use current close dates and stage definitions. Explain forecast conversion assumptions and stale opportunities.

List principal, interest, maturity, security interests, covenants, and conversion terms. Ask counsel to review consent and priority issues.

Maintain executed documents and amendments for loans, notes, SAFEs, and other financing arrangements; reconcile them to the schedules.

Coordinate required disclosures with counsel and distinguish resolved matters from ongoing issues.

Keep recent investor updates, the capital plan, and open financing questions organized. Agree who handles investor references.

Identify unpaid invoices, accrued obligations, and payment commitments. Connect them to the ledger and cash forecast.

Review terms, renewals, minimum commitments, cancellation provisions, and any commitments outside the current budget.

Maintain statements and completed reconciliations for every relevant account, using the same period as the financial package.

Label a compilation, review, or audit accurately. Ask the investor what level of work is required and agree the scope with your accountant.

Make the gaps a working plan.

For each missing item, name an owner, the underlying dependency, and the next action. Start with information that affects a current financing or operating decision. Confirm the investor’s requested scope before sharing company records.
