Treasury Hygiene for SaaS Founders

A practical review of cash access, payment approvals, account permissions, and finance ownership.

On this page

  • Five areas to review with your finance team.
  • Make deposit protection explicit.

Five areas to review with your finance team.

The aim is clear ownership, dependable access to operating cash, and controls the team actually follows. Complete the review with the people who approve payments, administer accounts, and reconcile the books.

1 · Cash access, coverage, and liquidity

Map every account, its purpose, restrictions, available balance, and settlement timing. Separate operating needs from reserves using your cash forecast. Ask providers to document deposit insurance arrangements and the risks of any investment product.

2 · Individual access and authentication

Give each authorized user an individual account with only the permissions they need. Enable strong multi-factor authentication, review recovery methods, and revoke access promptly when roles change.

3 · Independent payment approval

Define approval thresholds and an independent review for payments and changes to vendor banking details. Verify changed instructions through a known contact channel. Test the workflow with your bank’s approved process.

4 · ACH controls and reconciliation

Review authorized debit originators with your bank, investigate unknown transactions promptly, and reconcile recurring debits to current vendors and contracts. Assign an owner and review cadence.

5 · Card and spending controls

Define spending authority, limits, receipt requirements, and review responsibility. Select tools around the approval and accounting workflow. Reconcile balances and keep payment commitments in the cash forecast.

Make deposit protection explicit.

FDIC coverage is generally $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, per ownership category. Multiple accounts at the same bank do not automatically create additional coverage. Verify your entity’s account structure and any sweep arrangement rather than inferring coverage from a product name. FDIC: Understanding Deposit Insurance.

Compare liquidity, risk, fees, and current yield using provider documentation. Record who can make changes, how the decisions connect to runway, and when the policy will be reviewed.

A useful output: one treasury responsibility map.

For each account and payment flow, record the owner, approver, reconciler, backup, and escalation route. A control is useful when it is understood, followed, and tested.
