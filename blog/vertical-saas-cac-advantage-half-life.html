Testing the durability of a vertical SaaS acquisition advantage

Does the acquisition plan depend on a channel the company can repeat?

← Back to BlogConcept article · Content and factual review pending.

On this page

  • Test the source of the apparent advantage
  • Compare channels and cohorts consistently
  • Connect the evidence to the next investment

Test the source of the apparent advantage

A vertical SaaS company may acquire early customers through founder relationships or industry referrals. Whether that produces a durable acquisition advantage is a question for the company’s own data. An early blended CAC does not establish superiority to horizontal SaaS or predict future channel costs.

Compare channels and cohorts consistently

Define the costs included, allocation method, customer cohort, and period before comparing customer-acquisition costs. Track how founder involvement, referral volume, paid channels, and sales capacity change over time. Explain attribution gaps and small samples.

When evaluating payback, document the revenue and margin basis and the lag between acquisition spending and new customers. Compare like periods and cohorts rather than reading a shift in mix as proof that one channel became less efficient.

Connect the evidence to the next investment

Illustrative example: early wins come through the founder, while the next growth plan assumes a repeatable paid channel. Separate the historical relationship channel from the proposed channel and test its assumptions before applying the early blended CAC to the entire forecast.

A change in CAC might reflect mix, spending timing, contract values, retention, or attribution. Investigate the components before calling it the half-life of an advantage.
