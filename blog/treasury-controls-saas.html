Reviewing Treasury Controls Before a Board Conversation

Can the team demonstrate who can move cash and how exceptions are checked?

Review access, obligations, and account arrangements

A treasury review should explain where company cash is held, what is available for use, who can authorize movement, and how exceptions are detected. The review scope and duration depend on the accounts, products, and controls involved.

Distinguish a deposit from an investment

Identify the legal owner, bank, product, and any sweep arrangements for each balance. FDIC coverage depends on the insured institution and ownership category; opening another account at the same bank does not automatically create additional coverage. Money market deposit accounts and money market mutual funds are different products. The FDIC’s guidance explains which products are insured.

Use the bank’s product documents and current FDIC guidance to verify the actual arrangement. A yield feature does not by itself establish coverage, liquidity, or appropriate risk.

Check the controls in actual practice

Review authorized users, authentication, approval limits, vendor-payment changes, and exception alerts. Confirm whether a second approval is required and whether it is working as intended. Document who reviews access when people or responsibilities change.

Keep an account inventory, available-cash schedule, control owners, and a dated exception log. Agree a review cadence and revisit it after material changes. This is continuing operational work, not a one-time setup guaranteed to take thirty minutes.
