The Series A Data Room: Connect the Story to Its Evidence

Can an investor trace a reported number back to its evidence?

← Back to BlogConcept article · Content and factual review pending.

On this page

  • Organize a traceable evidence set
  • Build the bridge between headline and source

Organize a traceable evidence set

A Series A data room should help a reviewer connect the company’s financial story to current supporting records. Confirm the requested scope, access permissions, and document owners before sharing. Folder completeness does not establish financing readiness.

Build the bridge between headline and source

For each material claim in the presentation or model, locate the source schedule, measurement period, and definition. Recurring-revenue metrics need their own definitions and a bridge to accounting records; they need not equal period revenue in the P&L.

Keep financial statements, customer schedules, ownership records, and relevant agreements consistent by reporting cutoff. Track differences, restatements, and missing items instead of silently replacing files. The investor’s actual request determines the required history and review sequence.

Make the open items manageable

Create an index with document name, period, owner, current version, and open question. Ask counsel and the accounting team to review items within their responsibility. Keep sensitive personnel and contractual records under appropriate access controls.

Illustrative example: the deck and model use different customer start dates. Preserve the explanation, correct the source assumption, and identify which outputs changed. Reorganizing the folders alone will not resolve the inconsistency.

Use the fourteen questions and twenty-four organizing items in the diligence guide below to prepare the evidence review. Adapt the list to the requested process; it is not a universal investor checklist.
