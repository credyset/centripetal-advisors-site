The Series A Data Room: What Goes In and What Gets Opened

Can an investor trace a reported number back to its evidence?

The data room is not a filing cabinet

Most founders treat the data room as a storage project: collect the documents, organize the folders, share the link. That is necessary but not sufficient.

The data room is a verification environment. Investors open it to test whether the numbers in the deck, the model, and the conversation all describe the same company. The order they open things in is not random.

What gets opened first

Financial statements: the most recent monthly P&L, balance sheet, and cash flow. They compare these against the numbers in the deck and model immediately.

Customer-level revenue: the schedule that shows ARR by customer, by month, for as far back as records exist. This is where concentration, churn, and expansion become visible.

Cap table: to understand the current ownership structure and how the proposed round changes it.

What gets opened during diligence

AR aging and collections history. Employment agreements and IP assignments. Debt documents, if any. Material contracts, especially the top 5-10 customer agreements. The 409A valuation. Board minutes.

These are not opened on the first day. They are opened once the investor has decided the company is interesting enough to verify.

What most founders miss

A reconciliation document. Something that explicitly maps: here is the ARR number in the deck, here is the same number in the model, here is the source data in the customer schedule, and here is how it ties to the accounting records.

If an investor has to build that bridge themselves, they will. And they will find the discrepancies you did not know were there.

Frequently Asked Questions

What documents should be in a Series A data room?
Monthly financial statements for the past 24 months, customer-level revenue by month, cap table, material contracts, employment and IP agreements, AR aging, 409A, debt documents, and a reconciliation bridge between the deck and the source data.
What do investors open first in a data room?
Financial statements, customer-level revenue schedules, and the cap table. They compare these against the numbers in the deck and the financial model to test consistency.
What do most founders miss in their data room?
A reconciliation document that explicitly maps the ARR in the deck to the customer schedule, the model, and the accounting records. Without it, investors build the bridge themselves and surface discrepancies the founder did not catch.
