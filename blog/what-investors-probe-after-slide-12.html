Beyond the Pitch Deck: Financial Evidence to Prepare

Which links between the pitch story and supporting evidence need explanation?

Prepare for verification beyond the headline

After a financing presentation, useful preparation connects the headline metrics, operating assumptions, ownership records, and source evidence. The sequence and scope vary by investor and company; this article offers review questions rather than predicting what every investor will ask.

Start with four evidence bridges

Recurring revenue: state the ARR and CARR definitions, cutoff, and customer schedules. Explain the bridge to recognized revenue instead of assuming those numbers should match.

Customer growth: separate new customers, expansion, contraction, and churn using consistent periods and cohorts. Show material concentration and the contract timing behind it.

Forecast: connect the next operating commitment to its assumption, source, owner, and alternative case. Explain changes from actual performance.

Ownership: reconcile the capitalization schedule to the underlying instruments and approvals with the relevant specialists. Record any unresolved terms or missing records.

Turn questions into a review plan

For each gap, name the evidence needed and the person who can resolve it. Agree the version and reporting period before the documents circulate. A supported explanation of a difference is more useful than forcing unlike metrics to match.

The diligence guide below expands the evidence questions. Use it alongside the investor’s requested scope; neither a polished deck nor a completed checklist establishes funding or approval.
