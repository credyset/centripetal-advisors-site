CARR vs ARR: When the Definition Matters

Which definition and contract timing explain the gap between contracted and live recurring revenue?

← Back to BlogConcept article · Content and factual review pending.

On this page

  • Keep the metrics and accounting view distinct
  • State the cutoff and inclusion rules
  • Explain differences instead of forcing equality

Keep the metrics and accounting view distinct

ARR describes an annualized recurring-revenue run rate using a stated operating definition. CARR adds contractually committed future recurring revenue under a stated policy. Neither metric is the same as revenue recognized in the financial statements. Stripe’s explanation distinguishes these views.

State the cutoff and inclusion rules

Document what counts as active, which signed contracts or expansions are included in CARR, and how known cancellations or contractions are handled. Identify treatment of usage-based revenue, services, implementation fees, and contract start dates. Keep the same definitions across reporting outputs.

Build a customer-level bridge that distinguishes contracted value, activation, billing, recognized revenue, and cash receipts. A signed agreement does not imply that all five occur on the same date. Ask the accounting owner to confirm recognition treatment for the actual contracts.

Explain differences instead of forcing equality

Illustrative example: a subscription is signed before implementation starts. Depending on the documented metric policy, it may be included in contracted recurring revenue before the active run rate. Its accounting recognition and collection dates follow their own conditions.

Reconcile each metric to its source schedule and explain the bridge to the accounting view. There is no blanket 1–2% tolerance that makes differences acceptable, and ARR need not equal a period revenue line.
