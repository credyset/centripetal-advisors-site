CARR vs ARR: When the Definition Matters

Which definition and contract timing explain the gap between contracted and live recurring revenue?

Why the distinction matters

ARR measures what is live and recognized. CARR measures what is contracted, including deals signed but not yet live. When an investor asks for ARR, they are asking for the tighter number. When a founder reports CARR as ARR, the reconciliation breaks in diligence.

This is not a semantic issue. The gap between CARR and ARR is an implementation timeline, a revenue-recognition policy, and a credibility test. A clean answer requires knowing which number you are reporting, what the difference is, and when the difference closes.

Where the gap shows up

The gap is usually largest in companies with longer implementation cycles, multi-product upsells with staged rollouts, or contracts where billing starts before the customer is fully live.

When an investor sees ARR in the deck and a different number in the financial model or accounting export, the first question is whether the founder is using the same definition across all three. If the answer requires a spreadsheet that maps contracts to go-live dates, the data room needs that spreadsheet before the first partner meeting.

How to present both numbers

Report ARR as the primary metric. Report CARR as a secondary metric with an explicit bridge: which contracts are signed but not yet live, what the expected go-live timeline is, and what the historical conversion rate from signed to live has been.

Investors do not penalize companies for having a CARR-to-ARR gap. They penalize companies for not knowing the gap exists or for presenting the larger number without context.

The reconciliation test

Take your ARR number from the deck. Compare it to the ARR number in the financial model. Compare both to the recurring revenue line in the P&L. Compare all three to the customer-level revenue schedule.

If all four match within 1-2%, you have clean ARR. If they do not match, the work is not to adjust the slide. The work is to clean the definition, build the bridge, and document the policy.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between CARR and ARR?
CARR is contracted annual recurring revenue, including deals signed but not yet live. ARR is annual recurring revenue from contracts that are live, recognized, and generating revenue. The gap is the implementation pipeline.
Should I report CARR or ARR to investors?
Report ARR as the primary number. Show CARR as a secondary metric with a clear bridge explaining the contracts in implementation and their expected go-live timeline.
How do investors test whether ARR is accurate?
They compare the ARR in the deck to the number in the model, the accounting records, and the customer-level revenue schedule. If the number differs across any of these, they ask why.
