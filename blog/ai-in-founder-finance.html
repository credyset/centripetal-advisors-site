Reviewing AI-assisted finance work

Who checks and owns the decision supported by an AI finance output?

← Back to BlogConcept article · Content and factual review pending.

On this page

  • Define the task and the review
  • Keep the source beside the output
  • Assign the operating responsibility

Define the task and the review

AI assistance in finance should be evaluated task by task. A useful output needs a traceable source, a check against the underlying records, and an accountable person before it informs reporting or a decision. Capability and reliability depend on the system, data, configuration, and task.

Keep the source beside the output

For an invoice extraction, compare fields with the original invoice. For a variance explanation, verify the period, calculation, and supporting records. For a generated forecast, identify which assumptions came from history, which came from management, and which have no support.

An apparently complete answer can conceal a missing input. Treat a fluent narrative as something to review, rather than evidence that the calculation or interpretation has been verified. Avoid entering company information into a system unless its handling fits the company’s approved use.

Assign the operating responsibility

Illustrative example: an AI-assisted board summary describes a cash decline. The reviewer still needs to connect it to collections, payment timing, and the management response. Record the source schedules and unresolved questions beside the summary.

NIST’s AI Risk Management Framework provides a broader basis for evaluating and managing AI risks. The task checks here are editorial prompts for finance work, not a claim that any particular AI product is suitable or unsuitable.

Begin with a bounded workflow, a defined review step, and a way to track corrections. Use the finance-foundation resource below to identify the ownership and information gaps that automation would otherwise reproduce.
