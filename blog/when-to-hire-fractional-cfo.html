When to Hire a Fractional CFO

What changed in the company that creates a need for senior finance ownership?

← Back to BlogConcept article · Content and factual review pending.

  • Start with the unowned decision
  • Distinguish a leadership gap from a records gap

Start with the unowned decision

A fractional CFO may fit when recurring financial decisions need senior judgment and the company can define a workable part-time scope. Begin with the work, its owner, and the availability required; revenue alone does not establish the right staffing structure.

List the next hiring, spending, capital, and board decisions. For each, identify the source information, the person who interprets it, and the person authorized to decide. A current forecast without an owner for changing its assumptions can be a different gap from an overdue close.

Distinguish a leadership gap from a records gap

Separate transaction processing, reconciliations, close oversight, forecasting, and finance leadership. The existing team may need better handoffs, additional accounting capacity, senior interpretation, or a combination. Titles do not guarantee coverage.

Ask a prospective provider to explain which responsibilities they would own, how they would work with the accounting team, and how availability changes around a board meeting or capital event. Centripetal’s precise engagement scope should be confirmed in the conversation.

Write a scope you can evaluate

Use a short brief: decisions to support; recurring outputs; reporting cadence; existing team; approval boundaries; and response expectations. Compare a fractional engagement, a project, and an internal hire against the same brief.

Illustrative example: if the records are current but management cannot connect a hiring plan to collection timing, the need includes planning and interpretation. If balances cannot be reconciled, the brief also needs accountable accounting work. Neither situation can be resolved by an ARR threshold.
