Bookkeeper, Controller, CFO: What Your Stage Actually Needs

Which finance responsibilities are uncovered by the current team?

Map responsibilities before choosing a title

Bookkeeping, controllership, and CFO work cover different responsibilities, but actual roles overlap. A useful starting map separates recording transactions, owning the close and accounting process, and connecting financial evidence to management decisions. Confirm the scope rather than assuming the title defines it.

Find the missing handoff

Transaction work needs accurate source records and reconciliation. Close oversight needs policies, supporting schedules, review responsibilities, and a dependable reporting calendar. Finance leadership needs assumptions, interpretation, and a connection to the operating and capital plan.

A management recommendation can fail if the underlying balances are unresolved. Equally, reliable statements do not by themselves establish who owns the forward forecast. Document where information changes hands and which person is responsible for exceptions.

Compare coverage using one work list

Take a recent reporting period and the next decision. List the work, who performs it, who reviews it, and where no one is accountable. Include the time and specialist input needed for the actual company; avoid using a generic percentage of tasks as a hiring rule.

Illustrative example: a delayed close and a hiring decision may require both close oversight and planning support. Ask candidates how those responsibilities would be covered, including coordination with legal, tax, and accounting specialists. Management retains the company’s decisions and approvals.
