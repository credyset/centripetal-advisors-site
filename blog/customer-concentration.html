Customer Concentration: Connecting Exposure to a Decision

What operating decision changes if a material customer renews late or leaves?

← Back to BlogConcept article · Content and factual review pending.

On this page

  • Define the exposure before judging it
  • Show more than one denominator
  • Separate delayed payment from customer loss

Define the exposure before judging it

Customer concentration describes how much of a stated business measure depends on a limited number of customers. Review it alongside renewal terms, payment timing, delivery commitments, and alternatives. A universal percentage cannot establish whether the company is investable.

Show more than one denominator

State the period and measure used for the largest customer and selected customer groups. Recurring-revenue concentration, recognized-revenue concentration, and near-term receipt concentration answer different questions. Keep the customer identifiers consistent with the underlying schedules.

Add the contract and relationship context: renewal dates, termination provisions, implementation dependencies, disputed receivables, and relevant account history. Do not treat a long contract or a strong relationship as proof that a payment or renewal is certain.

Separate delayed payment from customer loss

A delay changes receipt timing. A loss can change future recurring revenue, collections, and delivery costs. Model the relevant case and identify the first commitment affected, the responsible owner, and the evidence that would change the response.

Illustrative example: a customer represents a modest share of annual recurring revenue but most of the cash expected before a payroll run. A revenue-share chart alone would not reveal that timing dependency.
