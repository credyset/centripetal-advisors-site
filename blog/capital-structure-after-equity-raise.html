After the Raise, Capital Structure Becomes an Operating Decision

How would another financing obligation change the post-raise operating plan?

← Back to BlogConcept article · Content and factual review pending.

On this page

  • Start with the use and the obligation
  • Read the terms into the cash plan
  • Compare the alternatives using the same assumptions

Start with the use and the obligation

Adding debt after an equity raise is a financing decision that needs company-specific analysis. Define the use of proceeds, compare alternatives, and understand the obligations in the proposed agreement before deciding whether the additional capital fits the operating plan.

Read the terms into the cash plan

Review draw conditions, fees, interest, repayment timing, maturity, security, warrants or other equity terms, reporting requirements, and any covenants actually present. Do not assume every facility has the same terms. Review the documents with counsel and the relevant finance specialists.

Place the proposed obligations beside collections and operating commitments. Model an explicit change in a material assumption, such as delayed receipts or a moved milestone, and identify when a commitment or reporting condition would need attention.

Compare the alternatives using the same assumptions

Show the operating plan with and without the proposed facility, including the cost and timing of alternatives. Keep hoped-for financing distinct from committed availability. Negotiating leverage and terms depend on the actual company, lender, and market; no timing rule guarantees a better offer.

Illustrative example: a facility funds a planned hiring step, but that step depends on a customer implementation. Compare the repayment calendar with the changed receipt date and review the agreement’s conditions before treating the proceeds as additional runway.

The venture-debt guide below supports a preparation discussion. It does not determine borrowing eligibility or whether a particular facility is appropriate.
