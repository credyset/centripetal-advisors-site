Cash-Flow Evidence for a Lender Conversation

Which obligations and receipt assumptions should accompany a lender cash model?

← Back to BlogConcept article · Content and factual review pending.

On this page

  • Prepare the obligation and its support
  • Separate timing from flexibility
  • Test the assumption that changes the commitment

Prepare the obligation and its support

For a financing conversation, connect the cash forecast to the commitments, receipt assumptions, and agreement terms behind it. The lender’s requested evidence varies; a preparation framework cannot predict eligibility or approval.

Separate timing from flexibility

Identify payments already committed and choices still open. Review cancellation provisions and actual decision authority before treating spending as reversible. Connect expected collections to billing milestones, receivables aging, payment behavior, and responsible owners.

Where an agreement contains covenants or reporting conditions, review the actual definitions, dates, and calculation rules with the responsible specialists. Model those conditions using the same assumptions as the operating plan. Do not infer them from a generic venture-debt checklist.

Separate the facility headline from spendable cash.

Prepare four views: cash already received; undrawn capacity and its conditions; dated interest, principal, and fee obligations; and the operating commitments those receipts would fund. Keep unavailable or conditional capacity out of the cash available for an immediate commitment.

Illustrative facility: a later draw depends on a customer milestone. Implementation slips, so the team must revisit both the assumed draw date and the hire it was meant to fund. A model showing the full facility as cash on day one would hide that dependency. The response might concern the hiring sequence or financing plan; it cannot be inferred from a headline runway number.

Test the assumption that changes the commitment

An explicit scenario should explain both the cash effect and the management response. A delayed receipt and a customer loss change different parts of the plan, so review them separately where relevant.

Illustrative example: a planned hire depends on a large receipt. Move the receipt date, identify the affected commitments, and document who can act before the payment is due. Record the remaining uncertainty rather than labeling the model lender-ready.
