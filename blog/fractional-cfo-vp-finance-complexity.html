Fractional CFO or VP Finance? The Decision Is Complexity, Not ARR

The question is usually phrased as a revenue threshold. "When should we hire a full-time CFO?" The answer founders get is often a number: $5M ARR, $10M ARR, after Series A. That is too simple. The real driver is complexity, not revenue. A $3M ARR company with a single product, one entity, and straightforward billing may not need a full-time finance executive for years. A $3M ARR company with multi-entity structure, revenue recognition complexity, debt covenants, and a board expecting institutional-grade reporting may need one yesterday.

Work Mix

The first question is what the role actually requires. At the Seed and Series A stage, the finance function typically includes: monthly close and reporting, board deck preparation, investor updates, cash flow management, financial modeling, fundraising support, and ad hoc strategic analysis. This is 10-20 hours per week of high-judgment work. A fractional CFO handles this directly. A VP Finance hire at this stage often means paying a full-time salary for part-time strategic work, with the balance filled by operational tasks that a bookkeeper or controller handles more efficiently.

Reporting Complexity

The inflection point is not revenue. It is reporting complexity. When the company has multiple entities, international operations, complex revenue recognition, or covenant compliance requirements, the daily cadence of financial management increases. A fractional CFO can handle substantial complexity, but when the role requires 30+ hours per week of consistent attention, a full-time hire becomes the right structure.

The Cost Comparison

An embedded fractional CFO typically runs $95K-$150K fully loaded per year. A VP Finance with comparable judgment and experience costs $220K-$320K loaded, before equity. The difference is not just cost. It is also the risk of a bad hire at a stage where the company cannot afford to get it wrong and start over.

When to Transition

The transition from fractional to full-time usually happens when three things converge: the weekly workload consistently exceeds 20 hours, the company needs someone managing a growing finance team, and the operating cadence requires daily cross-functional involvement. Most companies reach this point between Series A and Series B, but the timing varies with complexity, not with a specific ARR milestone.

The decision is not about what you can afford. It is about what the role actually requires. Match the structure to the work, not to a revenue benchmark that may not apply to your business.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should a SaaS company hire a VP Finance instead of a fractional CFO?
When the company needs a daily owner for a growing finance team, multi-entity reporting, covenant compliance, and recurring cross-functional operating cadence that exceeds 20 hours per week.
What does a fractional CFO cost compared to a VP Finance?
An embedded fractional CFO typically runs $95K-$150K fully loaded per year. A VP Finance with comparable judgment costs $220K-$320K loaded, before equity.
Can a fractional CFO handle board reporting and investor relations?
Yes. Board decks, investor updates, fundraising models, and cap table management are core fractional CFO work at the Seed through Series A stage.
